# Why free-match exists

## The pain (everywhere, not one country)

Across food delivery, ride-hailing, retail marketplaces, and local services, the same story repeats:

1. **High take-rates** — intermediaries charge large commissions, ads, and “service fees.”  
2. **Monopoly of discovery** — buyers and sellers only find each other *inside* one app.  
3. **Merchants and workers squeezed** — kitchens, shops, and drivers become dependent on ranking and rules they do not control.  
4. **Users pay twice** — higher menu/ride prices *and* less choice when platforms gatekeep.  
5. **Captive reputation** — leave the app, history resets; leave the rules, risk deplatforming.

People did not ask for a permanent landlord between every meal and every ride. They asked for **matching**.

## Why agents change the equation

General-purpose agents (LLM + tools + skills) can already:

- understand natural language (“find me vegetarian lunch within 2 km under 12 EUR”)  
- speak structured protocols  
- track threads, deadlines, and reminders  
- sit on the user’s device or co-op server — not only on a corporation’s cloud  

So the missing piece is **not** another closed marketplace app.  
It is an **open peer-matching protocol + skill** that any agent can load:

```text
merchant agent  ←→  free-match messages  ←→  buyer agent
                         ↕
                   courier / driver agent
```

No mandatory take-rate field. No paid pin. No software pretending to be the police.  
Quality is portable reviews. Law remains with public authorities and local duties of whoever actually trades.

## What free-match is for

| Audience | Outcome |
|----------|---------|
| Merchant / kitchen / shop | List goods with text + images; take bids; settle terms peer-to-peer |
| Buyer / passenger / diner | NL filter listings; negotiate; track order status with agent reminders |
| Courier / driver | Quote delivery or trips; update fulfill events |
| Community / co-op | Run a **dumb board** (mirror), not a rent-seeking platform |

## What free-match is not

- Not a promise that platforms disappear overnight  
- Not a scraper or unofficial client for commercial super-apps  
- Not a paid “boost your shop” product  
- Not a global enforcement engine for content law  

## One sentence

**Platforms overcharged for matching; agents can match peers directly — free-match is the open language for that.**
